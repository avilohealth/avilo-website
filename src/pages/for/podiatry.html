How a podiatry front desk runs

Six-weekly regulars, diabetic foot checks, and patients who phone.

A podiatry diary is built from recalls. General treatment patients come back every six to eight weeks for years, diabetic patients return on a schedule for foot checks, and a good part of the week is people the clinic has seen dozens of times. These patients are reliable, but their appointments were booked a long way ahead, and the reminder is often the first time they think about the date.

The patient base skews older. Many regulars do not reply to a text at all; they ring the clinic the next morning, or they simply turn up because they always turn up. Others reply with a full message, a question about orthotics, or a note about a sore toe or a change in medication. The desk needs to know which silences are a patient who will be there, which need a phone call, and which replies carry something the podiatrist should see before the appointment.

What matters most in podiatry

Three features built for a recall-based diary.

Morning call list

For a patient base that rings rather than texts, silence is the normal reply. The 8am list gives the desk every non-responder in appointment order, so regulars get the quick call they expect and a late text takes them off the list automatically.

Patient inbox

Older patients write in full sentences, and a clinical detail can sit in the middle of a yes. The inbox lifts anything with a question or a symptom to the top, so the podiatrist sees it before the patient arrives.

Monday cancelled-without-rebook list

A recall patient who cancels and does not rebook can fall out of a six-weekly cycle without anyone noticing. Every Monday Avilo lists last week's cancellations with no future appointment, sorted by practitioner, so the cycle is restored before it breaks.

Questions

What podiatry clinics ask.

Most of our patients phone instead of texting. What does Avilo do for them?

It tells you who they are. Any patient who has not replied by the morning goes on the call list, in appointment order, in the app and in the desk's email at 8am. When they ring, the desk ticks them off. If they text late, they drop off the list on their own. On the texts and calls plans, Avilo also answers the phone when the desk cannot, day or night.

A patient mentions a wound or a change in medication in their reply. Does Avilo answer?

No. Anything about symptoms, wounds, medication or whether to attend is marked as needing a person and goes to the top of the inbox. The podiatrist or the desk reads the full message and replies. Avilo AI never answers a clinical question.

Does Avilo send our recall reminders?

Cliniko keeps sending the reminders you have set up. On Confirm, Avilo reads the replies, nudges the patients who go quiet, and puts recall patients who cancel without rebooking on the Monday list. On Automate, Avilo AI also handles recalls by text, so patients who fall out of their cycle get a check-in without the desk making every call.

Can we see which recall patients cancelled and have not rebooked?

Yes. Every Monday Avilo lists the previous week's cancellations with no future appointment, sortable by practitioner or location, emailed to the desk and kept in the app. A patient drops off the list the moment they rebook.
