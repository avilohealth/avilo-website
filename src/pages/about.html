Why Avilo exists

The problem nobody warned me about.

I work in a busy practice. Every practitioner has heavy patient engagement, and keeping on top of who's responded to a reminder is a constant, tedious task. You're treating back-to-back all day. A patient texts back to reschedule, replying to the reminder that literally says "do not reply" because everyone messages these days, and you don't see it for hours.

They don't show up. You've got a gap in your diary. Worse, if a couple of days pass before anyone replies, the patient thinks you ignored them. Sometimes you can't even get them back. That's not just a missed appointment. It's revenue that could have easily been kept, and a patient relationship that's now a little colder than it needs to be.

I kept hearing the same thing from colleagues at other practices. The problem isn't Cliniko. Its reminder system is excellent. The problem is that when a patient replies, there's nothing built to catch it. Avilo Confirmations is that missing piece, and it's where every clinic starts.

Why I'm the one building this

A physio who can't stop tinkering.

I'm a physio first. But I've always loved technology and the way a small system tweak can give a whole team hours back. I'm at a clinic with no on-site reception, where physios check their own messages, so I built the first version of Avilo for me and my team, to take the admin burden off our plates between sessions.

What I didn't expect was how much better it made things for patients. Even practices that do have reception benefit. Receptionists stop watching incoming messages all day and get back to being the welcoming face patients actually came in for, focused on the things that need a person.

Avilo is built by a working physio, in a working clinic, for the people who do this every day.

What Avilo believes

Four principles that shape every decision.

01

Start where it's easy.

One API key, ten minutes, nothing for patients to notice. If the first step needs a project plan, it's the wrong first step.

02

Speed is care.

A patient who replies and hears nothing for six hours doesn't feel valued. They feel forgotten. Responding fast isn't a tech metric. It's how trust gets built.

03

Confidence beats coverage.

Avilo only handles what it's certain about. Anything ambiguous, clinical, or emotional stops the automation and pulls a human in. A confident "I don't know" is worth more than a confident wrong answer.

04

Built by physios, for allied health.

Every feature gets filtered through one question: does this give the team their time back? If it doesn't, it doesn't ship.
