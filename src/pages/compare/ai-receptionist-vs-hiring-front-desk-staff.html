The two options

One is a person. The other is a list of tasks.

Hiring adds a whole person. An AI receptionist adds capacity for a narrow set of repetitive jobs. They overlap on messages and the diary, and almost nowhere else.

Hiring front desk staff

A receptionist, part time or full time, who is at the desk during opening hours and knows your patients by name.

Does well

  • Greets patients, reads the room and settles the nervous ones
  • Takes payments, processes claims and chases unpaid invoices
  • Handles the unexpected: a late walk-in, a practitioner off sick, a delivery
  • Builds the relationships that keep patients coming back

Cannot do

  • Be at the desk at 7pm when the "can I move tomorrow?" texts arrive
  • Reply to forty reminder responses while a patient is standing at the counter
  • Work through leave, sick days or a resignation without cover being found
  • Scale down quietly in a slow week

An AI receptionist (the Avilo Assistant)

Software connected to Cliniko that handles the message traffic and the diary changes that flow from it. It does not stand at a desk.

Does well

  • Reads every reminder reply and colours the diary before the team arrives
  • Answers routine texts and reschedules into real gaps, around the clock
  • Fills cancelled slots from the waitlist and runs the recalls you approve
  • Never takes leave, and setup takes an afternoon rather than a recruitment round

Cannot do

  • Greet anyone, take a payment or manage the waiting room
  • Answer a clinical question. Those go to a practitioner, every time
  • Replace judgement on anything emotional or unclear. Those go to a person
  • Answer every call today. Voice is rolling out as an add-on

Being fair

When another receptionist is the better choice.

Software does not fix a physical gap at the desk. Hire a person when any of these is true.

  • The desk itself is understaffedIf patients queue to pay, the waiting room runs itself or practitioners are doing their own billing between sessions, you need a person on the floor.
  • Your bottleneck is not messagesCount where the admin hours actually go. If the answer is claims, stock, reports and the phone, an AI receptionist frees less time than you hope.
  • You are growing into a second room or siteNew capacity usually needs a new person in the building. Automate the message load later, once they are settled.
  • Your patients expect to know the person at the deskIn some communities the receptionist is the clinic's face. Keep that, and automate the work that happens behind it.

Questions

What clinic owners ask before deciding.

Is Avilo meant to replace my receptionist?

No. A receptionist greets patients, takes payments, manages the room and handles the unexpected. Avilo takes the repetitive message and diary work off that person so they can do the rest properly.

Which tasks should I hand over first?

Reminder replies. Reading them, colouring the diary and listing who has not answered is the most repetitive job on the desk, and Avilo Confirmations does it with no AI talking to patients. Most clinics start there and move up later if the message load warrants it.

What happens to messages that arrive after hours?

With Confirmations, a reply at 7pm is read and the diary updated so the team sees it first thing. With the Assistant, a routine request such as moving an appointment is answered and booked straight away. Anything clinical, emotional or unclear goes to the person you nominate, full thread attached.

How long does setup take compared with hiring?

Recruiting and training a receptionist usually takes weeks. Confirmations connects to Cliniko in about ten minutes. Smart Reminders and the Assistant are set up with you on a call, and the Assistant's knowledge base is written with your team before it sends anything.
