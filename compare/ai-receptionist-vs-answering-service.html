AI receptionist or answering service: which one suits your clinic?

One is a person reading from a script. The other is software reading your live Cliniko diary. Here is what each does well, what each cannot do, and when the other is the better choice.

The two options

They sound similar. They do different jobs.

An answering service puts a person on the phone when your team cannot be. An AI receptionist works mostly in text, against your Cliniko diary.

A human answering service

A team of operators, local or offshore, answers your clinic's number under your name, from a script you supply, and passes messages back to you.

Does well

  • Picks up every call within your contracted hours, with a human voice
  • Handles a flustered or upset caller with real patience
  • Takes a clear message and sends it on by email or text
  • Follows a script for the common questions: hours, parking, what to bring

Cannot do

  • Book, move or cancel an appointment in Cliniko. Most services only take a message
  • See your reminder replies, missed texts or waitlist
  • Know a patient's history or which practitioner they usually see
  • Cover texts at all. Patients who reply to a reminder are still your job

An AI receptionist (the Avilo Assistant)

Software connected to your Cliniko account that reads and answers patient messages and books against the live diary. Voice answering is rolling out as an add-on.

Does well

  • Answers texts in under a minute, day or night, in your clinic's tone
  • Reschedules into real gaps and writes the change back to Cliniko
  • Reads every reminder reply, colours the diary and frees the slot on a no
  • Offers a cancelled slot to the waitlist and runs the recalls you approve

Cannot do

  • Answer a clinical question. Ever. Those go to a practitioner
  • Read tone the way a person can. An unclear message goes to your inbox
  • Take every call today. Voice is rolling out to Assistant clinics now
  • Work outside your knowledge base. If the answer is not there, a person gets it

Being fair

When a human answering service is the better choice.

We would rather you pick the right tool than pick us.

  • Your phone is the problem, todayIf most of your missed admin is unanswered calls and you cannot wait for voice to roll out, a human service fills that gap now.
  • Your callers need a lot of nuanceSome clinics see a high share of distressed, elderly or non-English-speaking callers. A patient operator handles that better than any script or bot.
  • You do not use ClinikoAvilo is built for Cliniko clinics. If your diary lives somewhere else, the Assistant cannot book into it.
  • You want a person's name on the phoneFor some practices the human voice is part of the brand, and no automation should take that away.

Questions

What clinics ask when weighing the two.

Can an answering service book into Cliniko?

Most cannot. Operators generally do not have access to your diary, so they take a message for your team to action the next morning. A few will log in to a booking system if you hand over access, which is a decision about who sees patient data. The Avilo Assistant books directly against the live diary through the official Cliniko API.

Does the Avilo Assistant answer phone calls?

After-hours and overflow voice answering is an add-on that is rolling out to Assistant clinics now. Texts, reminder replies, rescheduling, the waitlist and recalls are live today. Ask on the demo and we will tell you exactly where voice is up to.

What happens when the Assistant is not sure what a patient means?

It stops. The message and the full thread go to your inbox, and whoever you nominate is alerted within a minute. It never guesses and never gives clinical advice. A staff reply pauses the Assistant on that conversation.

Can I use an answering service and Avilo together?

Yes, and some clinics do. The service covers the phone while Avilo covers texts, reminder replies and the diary. If the service logs a message asking to move an appointment, your team actions it in Cliniko and Avilo picks up the change from there.
