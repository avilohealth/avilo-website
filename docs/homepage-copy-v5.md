## 1 · Hero

**Unchanged from the current site.** Keeping the headline, subhead, CTA and the
animation, with additions noted below.

> ### The AI assistant that keeps your diary full.
>
> Avilo answers your patients by text and phone, books, moves and confirms
> appointments in Cliniko, and refills cancellations from your waitlist.
>
> **[ Book a demo ]**
>
> Works alongside Cliniko · Patient data hosted in Australia

**Animation additions** — both approved by Keegan, 22 Nov:

1. **Open on a slot being lost**, before anything gets filled. The sequence
   currently shows only wins, so the hero never states a problem. One lost slot
   at the start sets up section 3 and costs about two seconds.
2. **Add a fourth beat: a patient who cancelled sick being reactivated** and
   landing back in the diary, alongside the existing *slot freed → filled from
   waitlist → new patient booked.*

Net shape: **lose one → free one → refill from waitlist → reactivate a sick
cancellation → new patient booked.** Problem first, then four kinds of win.

## 3 · The leaks

> # The leaks that impact every clinic.
>
> It isn't a staffing problem. The work only shows up when someone has time to
> go looking for it, and nobody does.

**Four leaks.** Each one a name, then the mechanism, in the owner's language.

> ### No-shows
> A patient who doesn't reply to their reminder is telling you something. If
> nobody's chasing the silence, you're just hoping they turn up.
>
> ### Missed messages
> Your doors shut at six. The messages don't. Waiting until the next morning is
> usually too late to do anything about it.
>
> ### Missed calls
> Only about one in five callers leaves a voicemail. The rest ring the clinic
> down the road.
>
> ### The ones who said they'd rebook
> They were sick. They meant it. Then life got busy. If you don't reach out,
> they don't come back.

**The cost line, closing the section:**

> Every one of them is a patient you already paid to get. You're spending
> marketing money to replace people who were already yours.

**Note:** the dollar figure was cut on Keegan's call, 22 Nov. The argument is
stronger without a number the reader can argue with, and it avoids putting
invented maths on the page.

## 4 · What Avilo does for your clinic

> # Avilo is the system.
>
> Five jobs, every day, without anyone having to remember.

> ### Predicts no-shows
> Patients who don't reply to their reminder get nudged. Patients who reply N
> get offered a reschedule instead of just vanishing from the diary.
>
> ### Reactivates the ones who dropped off
> Checks in on patients who aren't in the diary any more, at the gap you set,
> in your clinic's voice.
>
> ### Works your waitlist
> The moment a slot opens, it's offered. You don't have to be awake for it.
>
> ### Never lets a patient feel ignored
> Texts and calls answered day and night, so nobody is left waiting on a reply
> that never comes.
>
> ### Sorts everything else
> Every reply triaged as it lands. Your team opens a list of three things that
> need a person, not twenty things that need reading.

**Note:** "Predicts no-shows" is written as a mechanic — non-reply is the
signal, the nudge is the action — rather than as a risk score. That's
defensible against what's actually shipped. If the product genuinely scores
risk, say so and this gets stronger.
