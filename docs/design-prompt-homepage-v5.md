## Who it's for

Owners of Australian allied-health clinics — physio, chiro, osteo, podiatry,
speech. Typically 2–10 practitioners. They are busy, sceptical of software, and
have usually been pitched an AI receptionist before.

**The single thing this page must do:** make that owner feel the page
understands their clinic better than the competition does. We are not winning
on scale — we have five clinics live. We win on insight.

---

## Visual direction

**Keep the existing brand.** Navy `#172030`, teal `#0D9488`, Plus Jakarta Sans,
white and `#F8FAFC` grounds. The current site's look is right; this is an
evolution, not a rebrand.

**Do not go purple.** Both direct competitors (lyngo.ai, onepatient.com.au) are
heavy violet with pastel gradient heroes. Navy and teal is the only
differentiated thing in the category.

**Restrained and editorial.** No pill badges, no pulsing status dots, no
uppercase micro-tracked eyebrows, no gradient meshes, no glassmorphism. The
clinic owner should read this as a serious Australian health business, not a
Silicon Valley launch page.

**Type does the work.** Blocks 3 and 4 are arguments, not product tours. They
should be carried by typographic hierarchy and white space. Resist the urge to
illustrate every line — the page already has two rich visuals (the hero
animation in block 1, the conversation replay in block 5) and a third fighting
them is what made earlier attempts fail.

**Mobile matters.** Roughly half the traffic. Blocks 3 and 4 are four and five
items respectively — they must stack cleanly without becoming an endless scroll.

## Block-by-block

### 1 · Hero — KEEP, with two animation changes

Headline, subhead, CTA and trust strip stay exactly as they are:

> ### The AI assistant that keeps your diary full.
> Avilo answers your patients by text and phone, books, moves and confirms
> appointments in Cliniko, and refills cancellations from your waitlist.
>
> **[ Book a demo ]**
>
> Works alongside Cliniko · Patient data hosted in Australia

**The animation currently runs:** slot freed → filled from waitlist → new
patient booked. It only ever shows wins, so the hero never states a problem.

**New sequence — five beats:**

1. **A slot is lost.** A patient texts, nobody sees it, the slot empties. This
   is new and it's the important one: it sets up block 3.
2. A slot is freed.
3. It refills from the waitlist.
4. **A patient who cancelled sick is reactivated** and lands back in the diary.
   Also new.
5. A new patient is booked.

Problem first, then four kinds of win. Keep it calm — this is a diary filling
itself, not a dashboard having a seizure.

### 2 · Proof strip

Directly under the hero. **Two numbers and one voice, roughly equal visual
weight** — the quote is not a footnote under the stats.

> **30%** — fewer no-shows at Running Room clinics after switching on reminders
> with confirmations
>
> **10,000+** — patient messages handled a month across Avilo clinics
>
> > "It saves our reception team at least 90 minutes a day. It sorts 500+
> > messages a week for us."
> > — **Andrew**, owner, Clinical Physio St Ives

Every claim is attributed on purpose. Don't strip the attribution lines to
tidy the layout — they're what makes the numbers believable.

### 3 · The leaks

> # The leaks that impact every clinic.
>
> It isn't a staffing problem. The work only shows up when someone has time to
> go looking for it, and nobody does.

Four leaks. Each has a name and a mechanism:

> **No-shows**
> A patient who doesn't reply to their reminder is telling you something. If
> nobody's chasing the silence, you're just hoping they turn up.
>
> **Missed messages**
> Your doors shut at six. The messages don't. Waiting until the next morning is
> usually too late to do anything about it.
>
> **Missed calls**
> Only about one in five callers leaves a voicemail. The rest ring the clinic
> down the road.
>
> **The ones who said they'd rebook**
> They were sick. They meant it. Then life got busy. If you don't reach out,
> they don't come back.

Closing line:

> Every one of them is a patient you already paid to get. You're spending
> marketing money to replace people who were already yours.

Then, at the close of the same block, the founder note — small, human, not a
hero moment:

> **Keegan Brady** · Physiotherapist and founder
> "I built Avilo after years of finding patient messages hours too late."
> Read the story →

**Design intent:** this is the most important new block on the page. It must
land as recognition — "that's my clinic" — not as an accusation. The founder
note directly beneath is what turns the list from marketing into testimony, so
keep them visually connected rather than separating them with a section break.

### 4 · What Avilo does

> # Avilo is the system.
> Five jobs, every day, without anyone having to remember.

> **Predicts no-shows**
> Patients who don't reply to their reminder get nudged. Patients who reply N
> get offered a reschedule instead of just vanishing from the diary.
>
> **Reactivates the ones who dropped off**
> Checks in on patients who aren't in the diary any more, at the gap you set,
> in your clinic's voice.
>
> **Works your waitlist**
> The moment a slot opens, it's offered. You don't have to be awake for it.
>
> **Never lets a patient feel ignored**
> Texts and calls answered day and night, so nobody is left waiting on a reply
> that never comes.
>
> **Sorts everything else**
> Every reply triaged as it lands. Your team opens a list of three things that
> need a person, not twenty things that need reading.

**Design intent:** blocks 3 and 4 are a matched pair — the leaks, then the
system that catches them. They should feel like two halves of one argument,
through shared rhythm or structure, without block 4 becoming a literal
mirrored table of block 3. Five items against four, so don't force a 1:1 grid.

> # Watch it happen.
> Real conversations, handled start to finish. Names changed.

Keep the "Based on a real conversation · names changed" caption inside the

>

> **[ Book a demo ]**

**This block carries the objection that kills demos** — *"it's just one more

Keep the existing **"Not on Cliniko?"** capture form in this block. It's
generating leads. Its inputs must keep their `name` attributes (see below).

### 7 · FAQ — KEEP, three copy edits

Existing ten questions and accordion stay. Edits:

  it's their only remaining mention on the page.
- **"How much does it cost?"** matters more now pricing is off the page. Full
  answer, link to `/pricing`.
- The Avilo AI price is **$99**, not $139.

## Technical requirements for the handoff

Three previous handoffs have been integrated into this site. Each one cost
hours of rework for the same avoidable reasons. Please:

- **All `src` and `href` must be root-relative** (`/assets/x.jpg`, not
  `assets/x.jpg`). The site has no `<base href>`, so relative paths 404 on
  every page below the root. This includes fragment links — `/demo#trial`, not
  `Demo.dc.html#trial`.
- **One `style` attribute per element.** Duplicates are silently discarded by
  browsers, and a card lost its entire layout this way last time.
- **Every form input needs a `name`.** A form shipped without them, `FormData`
  collected nothing, and every submission for weeks arrived empty.
- **Don't ship content inside conditional wrappers** that the converter will
  drop. Everything that should appear on the page must be in the markup, hidden
  with CSS if it starts collapsed. Six of seven FAQ answers went missing this
  way.
- **No `loading="lazy"` iframes inside hidden containers.** They never load.
- **Never write a price anchor of `$139`** against anything that isn't
  call-answering. Reminders and texting are from $49, anything using Avilo AI
  is from $99, calls answered from $139. The build fails on this.

**Phrases the build rejects outright** (a legal review found them untrue of
production): "AI front desk", "within 60 seconds", "web chat" (outside the FAQ
coming-soon mention), "not offshore", "escalation threshold", "in one click",
"two-factor", "two-step sign-in", "Officially integrated". Don't reintroduce
them.
